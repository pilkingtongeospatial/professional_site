# About Me

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/dusty-portrait.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Dusty Pilkington" style="max-width: 320px; border-radius: 8px; float: right; margin: 0 0 1rem 1.5rem;">

For the past 7 years, I have worked in the autonomous vehicle industry, first as a GIS Analyst, and now as a Software QA
Engineer — first at a startup called CARMERA, then at Woven by Toyota. I began in the autonomous vehicle world performing
quality assurance on lidar based High Definition maps, making sure the vehicle has the context it needs during
operation. I have moved into the simulation realm, where I work to expand metrics generation in an automated, end to end
QA system.

I got my start in the geospatial world and coding in my 3-year role as a Land Use Planner and GIS Technician for 
Kittitas County, WA. I applied GIS technology to multiple projects and policy issues. These projects include mapping
and analysis of valued agricultural land and solar energy feasibility buffers to inform the development of the
County's Solar Power Production Facilities ordinance. I coordinated a two-intern team in developing a
database of nearly 28,000 land parcels assessing eligibility for groundwater permitting and mitigation
under the Washington Supreme Court decision *State of Washington Department of Ecology v. Campbell &
Gwinn*. In my day-to-day workflows, I used Python automated mapping in reviewing land use applications,
updated and maintained the County Zoning GIS layer, and performed mapping and analysis on an as needed
basis.

This was after a Master of Science degree in Cultural and Environmental Resource Management at
Central Washington University, titled "Two Post-Glacial Fire Records at the Wildland-Urban Interface,
Eastern Cascades, Washington". The work involved using charred particle analysis to reconstruct fire
history going back up to 15,000 years, and to infer the relative contributions of climate and human
activities on fire activity. This thesis work was used to better understand the long-term frequency
and magnitude of fire events since the Late Glacial period, and inform land use and land management
policies in the Wildland-Urban Interface of the sagebrush steppe environments in Okanogan County, WA in
a warming world.
